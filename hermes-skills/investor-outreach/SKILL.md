# Investor Outreach

Write investor communication that is short, personalized, and easy to act on.

## Core Rules

1. Personalize every outbound message.
2. Keep the ask low-friction — one specific next step.
3. Use proof, not adjectives.
4. Stay concise — under 150 words for cold emails.
5. Never send generic copy that could go to any investor.

## Cold Email Structure

1. **Subject line**: Short and specific — not "Exciting opportunity"
2. **Opener**: Why this investor specifically — portfolio fit, thesis, specific post/talk
3. **Pitch**: What the company does + why now + one concrete proof point
4. **Ask**: One specific next step (30-min call, look at deck, etc.)
5. **Sign-off**: Name, role, one credibility anchor

## Personalization Sources

Reference one or more of:
- Relevant portfolio companies (why the fund's thesis fits)
- A public thesis, talk, post, or article the investor wrote
- A mutual connection
- A clear market or product fit with the investor's stated focus

If context is missing, ask for it or flag that the draft is a template awaiting personalization.

## Follow-Up Cadence

- Day 0: Initial outbound
- Day 4–5: Short follow-up with one new data point
- Day 10–12: Final follow-up with clean close

Do not continue nudging after three touches unless user requests a longer sequence.

## Warm Intro Requests

Make life easy for the connector:
- Explain why the intro is a fit in 2 sentences
- Include a forwardable blurb under 100 words
- Give the connector a clear out if it's not a fit

## Post-Meeting Updates

Include:
- The specific thing discussed
- The answer or update promised
- One new proof point if available
- The explicit next step

## Quality Gate

Before delivering:
- Message is personalized, not template-generic
- Ask is explicit and low-friction
- No fluff, no begging language
- Proof point is concrete (number, name, event)
- Word count is tight
